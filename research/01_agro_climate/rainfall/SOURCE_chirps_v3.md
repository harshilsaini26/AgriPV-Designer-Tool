| Known caveats and gaps | (1) Two weightings: area and cropland (see `../CLIMATE_INDICATOR_METHODS.md`). Cropland ÷ area annual rainfall: median 1.00 (range 0.37–1.63); the big shifts are hill districts whose farmland sits in drier valleys (East Khasi Hills 6,205 → 5,159 mm; Kullu 1,255 → 1,016 mm). Fallback to area weights: Lakshadweep (cropland cells fall outside CHIRPS data) and Sanchor (empty polygon). (2) v3 applies Legates–Willmott gauge-undercatch correction, so expect totals slightly **above** uncorrected gauge normals such as IMD's. (3) Satellite-infrared products struggle with orographic warm rain (Western Ghats, North-East) and give few stations in Ladakh. (4) Monthly data cannot produce rainy days (≥ 2.5 mm/day); that needs the CHIRPS v3 daily product (`v3.0/daily/`, ≈ 30× more data). (5) `ond_cv_pct` is unstable where OND rain is tiny (NW India) |
| Processing notes | `research/_scripts/chirps_district_normals.py` (rasterio / geopandas; district weights = cell fraction inside the LGD polygon, rasterised at 0.01°, × cos(latitude)). The script sets `GDAL_HTTP_VERSION=1.1` and a timeout because a first background run hung on the default HTTP path. Run 2026-11-06: 30/30 years, 784/785 districts. **Sanchor** (Rajasthan; abolished Dec 2024) has no cells, so its polygon in the Bharatlas layer covers no 0.01° sub-cell. Monthly normals sum to the annual normal for every district. Annual range 205 mm (Jaisalmer) to 7,322 mm; median 1,204 mm |
| Validation vs IMD | Name-matched against IMD district normals 1971–2020 (local QA only, see `SOURCE_imd_rainfall.md`): **552 districts matched; CHIRPS ÷ IMD median 1.14 for annual (IQR 1.05–1.22) and 1.13 for JJAS (IQR 1.04–1.23); 66% within ±20%.** So CHIRPS v3 here runs about **13% wetter** than IMD's gauge normals. Expected causes: v3's gauge-undercatch correction, the different window (1991–2020 vs 1971–2020), and IMD's unmasked gauge means. Biggest outliers: gauge-sparse hill districts (Kargil ×9.5, Kiphire ×2.3, Mon ×1.7, SW Khasi Hills ×2.9), where IMD itself is weak, and Delhi (×0.24–0.29), where CHIRPS gives a plausible 580–795 mm, so the IMD-side entry or name match is suspect. **Decision (owner, 2026-11-06): use CHIRPS as-is, with no rescaling.** The +13% wet difference against IMD gauge normals is documented here and should be stated on the app's methodology page. Seasonal shapes, CV and relative ranking are unaffected |
