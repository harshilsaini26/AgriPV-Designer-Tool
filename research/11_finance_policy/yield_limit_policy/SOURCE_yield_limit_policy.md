| Known caveats and gaps | (1) **No Indian rule exists yet.** Nothing published states the spec's 80%. The nearest are the Alliance's 10–20% loss cap (2024) and PFI's call for season- and crop-specific thresholds (2025). MNRE announced a draft policy in October 2026, but no text was found by 2026-11-14. (2) **Primary text was read for India (Alliance, PFI), Japan, France and New Jersey.** Germany's DIN SPEC 91434 is paywalled (figures from Fraunhofer ISE and law-firm summaries), Italy comes from project verification documents, and Taiwan and Austria come from the Alliance's summary. (3) **One secondary claim was not confirmed.** PFI 2025 says Italy caps yield loss at 30% with a minimum height of 1.3 m. The 2022 guidelines, as summarised in project documents, set no yield threshold, and 1.3 m is their livestock height (crops need 2.1 m). (4) **Massachusetts figures are unverified.** They come from draft guideline text found by search (mass.gov refused automated access): a lowest panel edge of 8 ft (fixed) or 10 ft (trackers), and shading at most 50% of baseline. (5) Japan's rule was reported as tightening in 2026 (a shading-rate rule); that report could not be opened (HTTP 404) |
| Processing notes | PDFs were read with PyMuPDF text extraction (Japanese text included). Japan's 2024 rule: MAFF moved the permit criteria from a 2013 bureau-director notice into the Agricultural Land Act Enforcement Regulations, effective 1 April 2024. A permit is refused if unit yield falls by 20% or more against the regional average for the same crop and year, or if clearance is below 2 m. Annual reports are required, and violators lose FIT/FIP payments. At the end of FY 2022, 22% of facilities (927) had farming problems, two-thirds of them yield loss or poor growth. France's control zone must cover at least 5% of the installed area (at most 1 ha) |
