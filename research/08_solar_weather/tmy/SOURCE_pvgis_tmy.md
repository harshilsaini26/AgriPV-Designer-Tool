| Known caveats and gaps | (1) **Total sunlight is close nationally but biased regionally.** The median district is −0.9% against GSA, but **237 of 785 districts are outside ±5%**. The typical year is too sunny across the Indo-Gangetic plain (Delhi +11%, Punjab and Haryana +7%, Uttar Pradesh +6%; 43 of 75 UP districts flagged) and in Ladakh (+23%). It is too dull in the wettest and hilliest states (Kerala −7%, Sikkim −7%, Meghalaya −6%). This fits ERA5's known weak representation of winter haze and fog over the plains and of monsoon cloud. (2) **Diffuse light is far too low.** Median diffuse share is **0.31** in the typical year against **0.49** in the Global Solar Atlas and **0.45** in NASA POWER (`../solar_atlas/`). Annual diffuse is 37% below GSA in the median district. ERA5 sends too much light as direct beam, which would exaggerate shadows under panels and understate diffuse light on crops and module backs. **Owner decision (2026-11-14): calibrate to the Global Solar Atlas** (see processing notes). (3) **Leh's point was moved by hand.** The official Leh district polygon includes Gilgit-Baltistan, and the WorldCover cropland centroid fell there (35.61° N, 74.62° E, 1,296 m). Leh now uses 34.13° N, 77.55° E (Indus valley farmland near Leh town, 3,242 m; `POINT_OVERRIDES` in the script). Ladakh still differs most between sources (raw typical year +16% for Leh and +20% for Kargil against GSA), and both sources are uncertain at that altitude, so both districts are flagged. (5) **Calibration fixes annual totals, not seasons.** One GHI factor per district cannot remove a seasonal bias, such as ERA5's missing winter fog over the plains; the calibrated winter months there may still be too bright. After calibration the monthly diffuse share sits 0.04–0.08 above NASA POWER in every month (it was 0.09–0.14 below), the same gap as between GSA and NASA POWER annually (4) A typical year has no year-to-year variability; P90-style risk needs the full 2005–2023 series |
| Processing notes | `research/_scripts/pvgis_tmy_districts.py` (stdlib fetch with retries; GSA sampled with `sample_gsa_districts.sample`). Checks: Jodhpur gives 2,024 kWh/m²/yr against GSA 2,032 (−0.4%), with a diffuse share of 0.27 annually (0.22 in December, 0.44 in July). **Calibration** (`research/_scripts/calibrate_tmy_to_gsa.py`), hour by hour, keeping PVGIS's timing, temperature, humidity, wind and pressure: GHI′ = k × GHI, with k = GSA annual GHI ÷ typical-year annual GHI (median 1.009, 5–95% range 0.92–1.07). Beam′ = c × k × (GHI − DHI) and DHI′ = GHI′ − beam′, with one factor c per district that makes annual diffuse equal GSA's (median 0.75: a quarter of the direct beam becomes diffuse; range 0.61–1.12). DNI′ = DNI × beam′ ÷ beam, which keeps each hour's sun geometry. Results: annual GHI and DHI match GSA in all 785 districts; the median diffuse share rises from 0.31 to 0.49; annual DNI falls by about 27%; no hour needed capping. 21 districts were scaled by more than 10% (`ghi_scaled_over_10pct`): the Delhi districts (k about 0.89), Nuh, hill districts (Kishtwar, Tehri Garhwal, Gangtok, Kinnaur) and some in Kerala, Karnataka, the north-east and Ladakh. A first version scaled diffuse by a constant instead; it hit the GHI ceiling in about 1,100 hours per district and gave implausible monsoon diffuse shares (0.65–0.76), so it was replaced. **Weather packs** (`pipeline/src/pipeline/weather/build_packs.py`): PVGIS stamps each hour on the hour, but its ERA5 irradiance is the mean of the hour that follows (`irradiance_time_offset` 0.5 h in every response). Each calibrated file therefore gets the sun's apparent zenith and azimuth (pvlib) half an hour after the stamp, or at the centre of the sunlit part of an hour with sunrise or sunset. Direct normal irradiance is re-derived as (GHI − DHI) ÷ cos(zenith), capped at the extraterrestrial value; the DNI that PVGIS publishes (and `dni` in the calibrated files) is normalised with the sun at the stamp, which inflates mornings and deflates evenings, and is not used. Annual DNI in the packs is 4% lower in the median district than in `tmy_calibration.csv`. Negative wind speeds (13 districts) are set to zero. The pack keeps the hours with GHI above zero (about 4,400 per district), stored as scaled integers (0.1 W/m², 0.1 °C, 0.01°). A first build (2026-11-14) took the sun at the stamp; a review found this skewed east- and west-facing results by up to 17%, and the packs were rebuilt on 2026-11-15. The builder checks each pack: annual GHI and DHI against `tmy_calibration.csv`, direct and sky light adding up, direct light within the extraterrestrial value, and low-sun clearness alike in the morning and afternoon (which fails when sunlight and sun are out of step); all 785 passed. Optimum tilt comes from `research/_scripts/gsa_opt_tilt_districts.py` (Atlas layer OPTA; no district needed the latitude fallback) |
