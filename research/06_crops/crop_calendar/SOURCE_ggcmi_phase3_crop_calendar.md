| Units and key column meanings | `dist_lgd`, `district`, `state_lgd`, `state`; `ggcmi_crop` (ri1 / ri2 rice main and second season, swh / wwh spring and winter wheat, mai, mil = bajra, sor = jowar, bar, bea, pea, soy, nut = groundnut, rap = mustard, sun, cot, sgc, pot, cas = tapioca); `system` (irrigated / rainfed); `planting_doy`, `maturity_doy` (day of year, cropland-weighted **circular** mean, so dates that wrap past 31 Dec average correctly) and the same as `MM-DD`; `season_days` (weighted mean growing-season length); `date_spread` (0 = every cell in the district agrees; > 0.05 ≈ cells differ by a month or more; 7% of rows); `data_source` (GGCMI's own source code, weighted majority: IndiaAgStat, RiceAtlas, MIRCA2000, SAGE, …); `area_weight_fallback` (district had no cropland, area weights used); `qa` (see caveats) |
| Known caveats and gaps | (1) **GGCMI fills every land cell**, so a district gets dates for crops it does not grow (cotton in Anantnag); join with crop area (D10) before use. (2) Quality depends on the underlying source. Cotton, maize, bajra, mustard, sugarcane, wheat and pigeon-pea-like `pea` come from Indian agricultural statistics, and rice from RiceAtlas: `qa = ok`. Rows built from global MIRCA2000 / SAGE data are `ok_lower_confidence`. (3) Spot checks against Indian practice in 9 districts (Ludhiana, Agra, Patna, Indore, Rajkot, Nashik, Jaipur, Thanjavur, Purba Bardhaman): rice, wheat (`swh`), maize, bajra, jowar, soybean, mustard, cotton and rabi pulses (`bea`, Oct/Nov to Feb/Mar) look right. **Potato is wrong** (Jul–Nov in the plains; real Oct–Mar), so `qa = do_not_use`. **Rainfed groundnut** in MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Rajasthan gives the rabi window (Oct–Feb), not the main kharif crop, so `qa = check`. Tapioca and sunflower have no Indian source (`check`). Winter wheat (`wwh`) ends too early in UP (Feb); use `swh`. Patna rice (Aug–Dec) runs about a month late. (4) No separate chickpea, lentil, moong, urad or tur; no onion, tomato, guar, sesame or coconut. These need a curated calendar (see `SOURCE_CANDIDATES.md`). (5) **MIRCA-OS (Kebede et al. 2024, CC BY 4.0) was reviewed and not used.** It has 666 Indian district units, but its months are state-level and wrong for India: irrigated wheat in Punjab, Haryana and UP is planted in June and matures in October, and Punjab rice runs April to July |
| Processing notes | `research/_scripts/ggcmi_district_crop_calendar.py` (rasterio, geopandas, netCDF4). Districts are rasterised on the 0.01° WorldCover cropland-fraction grid; each 0.01° cell takes its 0.5° GGCMI value. The default season windows in the model (kharif Jun–Oct, rabi Nov–Mar, zaid Mar–Jun) can now be refined per district for the crops marked `ok` |
